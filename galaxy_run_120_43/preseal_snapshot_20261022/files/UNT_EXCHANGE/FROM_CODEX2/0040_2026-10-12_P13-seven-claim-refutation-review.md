## 1. Halo comparisons

Evidence: HALO_PROTOCOL, HALO_AUDIT, CM_AUDIT, HALO_FRONTIER, RAR_TRANSFER, LADDER.

The radial protocol explicitly freezes **galaxy-balanced velocity MSE as the primary outer metric**: average radii within galaxies, then galaxies equally. It does not use a majority-of-galaxies rule to determine that metric. The completed 94-galaxy comparison reports network 285.872069, NFW 988.661224 and ISO 1702.299750 (km/s)². The frontier calls this lower *average* error, discloses that it is a development prediction, and denies established general superiority. That precise mean comparison is supported.

The audit's cautions are also supported. Network wins only 41/94 against ISO (53 losses); its paired median difference is +14.994153 (km/s)², favoring ISO. Five largest improvements contribute 85.48% of the NFW aggregate advantage and 96.07% of the ISO advantage. However, removing those five as a post-hoc diagnostic still leaves negative mean differences, -107.7969 and -58.7396 respectively. Thus “driven by five” describes concentration of the advantage; it does not establish that the mean improvement disappears without them, that the frozen metric was selected after the result, or that all five are simply one identical optimizer failure. Boundary and extrapolation weaknesses are documented separately.

The c–M comparison is a **different fixed-prior comparator**, not the unconstrained ISO comparison. Network wins 60/94 against central c–M NFW, with paired median -87.223653; the two fixed scatter arms give 67/94 and 61/94, with negative paired medians as well. LADDER's one-page statement specifically says c–M halos and approximately two-thirds of galaxies. That statement cannot be refuted with the 41/94 result against a different comparator.

The RAR caveat is confirmed: radial mean 285.221205 versus network 285.872069; original SPARC transfer scatter 0.201590 versus 0.201573 dex, with no demonstrated advantage over RAR. The effective adopted closure is not uniquely identified as native record dynamics.

Suggested correction: “The fixed outer-radius test favors the network on its preregistered mean velocity MSE, with much of that advantage concentrated in five halo extrapolations. It loses to unconstrained ISO in a majority of galaxies; a separate c–M NFW test favors it by mean, median and majority. It does not improve meaningfully on RAR or establish general superiority over halos.”
