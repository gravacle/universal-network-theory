# Imported assumptions, scales, and alternatives

Purpose: keep imports explicit and challengeable. Agreement with convention
is not itself proof; disagreement with convention is not itself evidence.
An import is licensed only at its stated scope. A successful standard-model
description at one scale is not automatically a microscopic law for UNT.

| Import used here | Exact role and domain | What is not inferred | If UNT takes another route |
|---|---|---|---|
| Finite Hilbert spaces, density operators, partial trace and common CPTP continuation | Mathematical structure of the currently frozen finite model and the new sufficiency lemma | That all fundamental record processes must be quantum channels, or that the model is realized in nature | Define the alternative state/evolution/readout structure; rederive the sufficiency criterion there and test its physical predictions. |
| Trace distance and operator norms | A finite mathematical difference diagnostic; its operational reading uses the permitted quantum measurements | A physical timescale, statistical significance, spacetime distance, or accessibility of every optimizing measurement | Specify accessible probes and use their observable differences; retain exact mathematical bounds separately. |
| Continuous Hamiltonian interpolation | A declared assumption of the local short-time response lemma | Equivalence to the frozen discrete admission schedule, locality, or long-time persistence | Prove the interpolation correspondence, or derive a discrete-time bound directly. |
| Thermodynamic/continuum language | A proposed limit requiring an inter-size/refinement map and controlled error | That independently constructed finite L values are already samples of one proved continuum theory | Construct another limit or a directly discrete observational map and state its validity scales. |
| Effective GR and an FRW background | One explicitly conditional dark-sector screening branch; assumes a common metric and homogeneous/isotropic background at the scales modeled | That GR, homogeneity, or the fluid map has been derived from finite records | Supply alternative background/source equations and their weak-field/observational limits; compare branches without smuggling in GR's conclusion. |
| Component continuity with exchange Q | Effective-fluid stress accounting in that branch, with a declared sign convention and all recipient/donor components | A globally conserved comoving energy in an expanding universe, separate conservation of every component, or a microscopic universal conservation axiom | State the actual symmetry/balance law, additional degrees of freedom and exchange; make the enlarged model closed and testable. |

| Cosmological observations and fitted parameters | Empirical inputs/comparators with data/model/scale provenance when actually used | A measured ratio is a derived UNT output, or a fit validates an ontology uniquely | Freeze the new prediction and its observational map, expose calibration, and use independent comparisons. |

## Evidence discipline for unconventional proposals

A departure should name the precise standard assumption it replaces, supply
a definite replacement mechanism, and yield consequences in an observationally
specified regime. It need not import a standard model outside that model's
earned domain. It must still account for the observations it claims to
explain, including quantitative limits where applicable. These are empirical
obligations, not a demand to preserve a conventional interpretation.

Likewise, a standard effective theory may be deliberately used as a temporary
benchmark without being adopted as fundamental. Keep the conditional branch
label attached to every resulting success or failure. In particular, the
no-acceleration result for the candidate dust-token/radiation maintenance
model applies to its specified positive-density effective-GR branch. It is
not a theorem that all record-based theories or alternative gravitational
dynamics cannot accelerate.

For every new test card, list: imported object; source/domain; units and scale;
assumption versus derivation; proposed extension; validation required for that
extension; and which conclusion would change if the import were withdrawn.
The register itself does not validate any import. It makes those obligations
visible so the fastest tests can challenge them directly.
