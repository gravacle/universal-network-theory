# Fast tests that can change our confidence

This is the recommended near-term priority after the user's clarification:
construct testable candidates and seek inexpensive, discriminating evidence
first; develop definitive broader proofs where the results justify them.
No probability that UNT is correct is inferred from a count of passed tests.

## A small pass is valuable only for the question it tests

The current finite evidence increases confidence that the declared model and
its implementation exhibit the reported behavior. Independent reconstruction
reduces coding error. Robustness tests address fragility to the selected
construction. None alone distinguishes UNT from ordinary quantum models in
which joint correlations affect later reduced dynamics.

For a proposed test, write down both `UNT candidate predicts ...` and `named
alternative predicts ...`. If both predict the same outcome, the test may be
a necessary consistency check but offers little discrimination. A positive
result should be evaluated together with its strongest plausible alternative,
not just against zero. For model-specific response, an appropriately defined
marginal-sufficient channel is one exact alternative; it is not all of
standard quantum mechanics.

Useful early failures are cheap: lack of energy conservation, an unselected
normalization, an unstable candidate, inconsistent probe metrics, or a
vanishing registered response can redirect work before a large computation.
These failures apply to the tested construct, not automatically to every
possible version of UNT. Success on those screens earns the next test, not
the final ontology.

## Minimal prospective test card

Before a new result is accessible, record:

1. the exact candidate rule and the named alternatives;
2. the quantity predicted, units, domain, and all free parameters/initial data;
3. the derivation and what existing data it used;
4. source/input identities and independent implementation or review plan;
5. acceptance, rejection and unresolved regions, with numerical/statistical
   uncertainty distinguished from effect size;
6. maximum compute cost, checkpoint loss window, and stop condition;
7. the interpretations permitted for each outcome.

The earlier dark-sector draft already exposed its abundance targets. This
development cannot be called genuinely blind with respect to those numbers.
Keep a complete attempt log, assess tuned matches honestly, and reserve a new
observable or future data for a genuinely held-out physical test. An arbitrary
half-L4 response floor, if explored, is only a disclosed engineering/research
criterion—not a physically derived threshold or proof that UNT is correct.

## Immediate recommendation

Prioritize the dark-sector closure/selection laboratory here while the other
Mac qualifies the L6 response code. These fronts are complementary: one tests
whether a potentially dramatic implication can become a predictive model;
the other extends the strongest existing mechanism evidence. Keep the
common-metric/source join as the central gravity bottleneck. Defer expensive
cosmological fitting until a model predicts enough to make failure possible.
