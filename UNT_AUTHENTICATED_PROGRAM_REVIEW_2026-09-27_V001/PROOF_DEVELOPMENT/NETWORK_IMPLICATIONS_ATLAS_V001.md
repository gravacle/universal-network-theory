# Network implications atlas

Date: 2026-09-27. Status: **CONSTRUCTIVE RESEARCH MAP — CONDITIONAL PATHWAYS, NOT RESOLVED PHYSICS**.

The network hypothesis has substantial explanatory potential if relations, states, and their changes generate the regularities currently described as spacetime, matter, and forces. Its strongest possible payoff is one mechanism explaining several phenomena together. Calling the universe a network does not yet choose that mechanism: the same adjacency graph can support different state spaces, update laws, energies, probabilities, and observable procedures, producing incompatible physics.

A candidate therefore needs primitive objects, a state space, dynamical rules, record/readout definitions, and a map between scales. Those need not presume a physical space containing the network. Nodes might represent events, subsystems, or records; these alternatives make different commitments. A directed connection is not automatically a causal influence, graph distance is not automatically measured distance, and operation count is not automatically physical energy.

Below, **current support** refers to named UNT evidence; **mechanism** describes a possible explanation; **test** identifies a relatively small discriminating experiment or proof task. An effective theory may be used conditionally where its premises are stated. Alternative microscopic or macroscopic branches remain possible, but must specify their replacements and observable consequences. No puzzle below is declared solved by the ontology alone.

## 1. Space, dimension, and locality

**Mechanism.** Operational space could be the structure inferred when signals, transport, and stable clocks repeatedly compare network events. A low-dimensional local description would then require special constraints on connectivity and dynamics: arbitrary graphs need not admit one consistent smooth geometry. Dimension should emerge from agreed operational scaling, rather than from drawing the graph in three dimensions.

**Current support.** UNT has finite causal/ordering constructions and conditional reconstruction routes. The [foundational dependency map](../../DEVELOPMENT_UNT_FOUNDATIONAL_PREMISE_DISCHARGE_V001/FOUNDATIONAL_DEPENDENCY_DAG.md) identifies missing probe completeness, calibrated volume, common probes, and refinement. The owner-once prism supplies a chosen finite interaction graph; it does not derive physical three-dimensional space. The fixed-finite-direction obstruction excludes one route to a smooth cone, not every discrete alternative.

**Test.** Freeze two independently defined probe types on one parent. Compare their inferred neighborhoods, travel times, and dimension under a small family of refinements. Disagreement rejects that common-geometry proposal. Agreement motivates a correspondence proof. A directly discrete observational theory is also admissible; it must explain which continuum-looking regularities its probes reproduce and where they differ.

## 2. Causality, propagation, and clocks

**Mechanism.** A network can distinguish an allowed connection from an influence actually transmitted by the update law. Locality becomes a bound on changes detectable outside an operational neighborhood. Duration can be defined through correlations between repeatable internal processes rather than assuming the program's iteration number is physical time.

**Current support.** UNT's owner-once chronology and finite influence/ordering lemmas make causal ancestry explicit. They do not establish a universal physical signal cone or common clock. The same-model dynamical-exponent result is conditional on LL-P; the finite ARGER `z=1` classification is a separate adopted criterion.

**Test.** Perturb one native input, bound the earliest distinguishable response elsewhere, and compare clocks built from two different observables. Also vary the order of updates that the candidate declares causally independent. A changed observable would expose hidden scheduler dependence. If a preferred ordering is physically fundamental instead, declare it and derive a discriminator rather than concealing it as an implementation choice.

## 3. Why gravity could depend on retained history

**Mechanism.** Retained correlations could change future propagation; if independently defined matter and clock probes then agree on one altered geometry, the change might become a geometric response. This is a sequence of mechanisms, not an identification of memory with curvature.

**Current support.** The accepted L4 revisit demonstrates a carrier response despite equal separate input marginals. The [lineage-response lemma](LINEAGE_RESPONSE_LEMMA_V001.md) identifies the exact condition: the continuation must detect a joint-correlation direction. The L8 formation-order curvature test was null. PMICS supplies curvature-symbol capacity and GL6AV a partial collective-response bridge. The macroscopic Einstein–Hilbert endpoint follows under explicit RGRL/WTC premises, including a common physical metric and complete stress/constraints.

**Test.** First isolate which correlation the frozen continuation reads; then compare two probes under matched KEEP/BREAK interventions with ordinary energy, preparation, and source channels controlled. A single-probe change could be an ordinary interaction. Agreement across independently defined probes plus reciprocal response would support the geometric interpretation. A nonmetric gravitational branch would instead need its own source/response law and observational map.

## 4. Time's arrow and entropy

**Mechanism.** A retained record can make histories operationally distinguishable. Irreversible-looking behavior could arise when accessible variables lose track of correlations or when records consume a physical resource. This potentially connects memory, thermodynamics, and the experienced direction of time, but those are distinct claims.

**Current support.** UNT establishes finite record and lineage mechanisms. It does not derive a cosmological arrow or a uniquely selected initial condition. Within the presently assumed unitary framework, global von Neumann entropy is invariant while subsystem entropy and accessible information can change. A different fundamental dynamics would require its own balance law.

**Test.** Compare forward/reversed preparations under the same declared readout and account for reservoirs and inaccessible variables. Determine whether an apparent arrow survives matching the boundary conditions, or was put in through a special initial state, discard operation, or scheduler. Derive a heat/work relation before treating information entropy as thermodynamic entropy. A successful finite retention mechanism alone would not explain why the universe began in its observed thermodynamic condition.

## 5. Quantum correlations, measurement, and probabilities

**Mechanism.** The network might explain why stable observations are relational records and why reduced descriptions omit physically relevant joint information. A deeper branch could seek the origin of the probability and composition rules themselves.

**Current support.** The owner-once model already assumes Hilbert spaces, amplitudes, density operators, and quantum readout. Its successful correlation tests operate inside that mathematical structure. [AURFT](../../LANE_RFT_AXIOMATIC_URFT_CLOSURE_V001/THEOREM.md) proves coverage inside adopted U-DCL; it explicitly does not derive outcome selection or Born probabilities. Reusing the assumed probability rule cannot derive that rule.

**Test.** Separate record stabilization from outcome/probability selection. Give a candidate explicit multi-outcome and incompatible-readout protocols, then ask whether its probability assignments follow from native dynamics or remain free. If a nonquantum network is proposed, specify its alternative state composition and measurement rules before comparing interference/correlation behavior. The existing L4 effect motivates studying retained joint information, not a claim to have solved measurement.

## 6. Matter, stable excitations, gauge structure, and charge

**Mechanism.** Matter could be persistent mobile excitations or defects of a network phase. Topology might protect certain distinctions; dynamics would determine stability, propagation, scattering, and effective masses. Gauge structure could arise from a redundancy or constrained physical state space, while conserved charge requires an actual dynamical conservation law.

**Current support.** UNT includes protected finite record constructions and partial source/charge/energy lanes. Its homological-capacity results are bounded; the old equation of homology with gravity was superseded. No cited current closure derives the observed particle spectrum from the network premise.

**Test.** For one explicit parent, identify a candidate excitation and follow its propagation and decay under allowed perturbations. For Hamiltonian dynamics, check whether a proposed charge commutes with the Hamiltonian; for a general update, use its appropriate adjoint conservation condition. In either case test whether evolution preserves the declared constraints. Compare with a same-size control lacking the proposed protecting structure. Protection, mobility, and charge together would be more informative than a suggestive mode count. Imported quantum/gauge machinery must remain labeled until its emergence is separately established.

## 7. Why particular constants might be selected

**Mechanism.** A network phase could restrict dimensionless couplings through symmetry, consistency, or an attractive flow. A successful selection theorem would explain why multiple allowed microscopic choices lead to one observable value, rather than importing that value as a calibration.

**Current support.** UNT's alpha result distinguishes construction-scoped ALLOW from conditional same-domain REQUIRE and open SELECT. The observed domain coupling is an input to inheritance. G is calibrated in the conditional working closure; Lambda remains free. The finite majority margin is not automatically an energy, coupling, or mass defect.

**Test.** Vary every still-allowed dimensionless parameter and initial condition while holding the claimed defining properties fixed. Two candidates admissible within the declared candidate class, with different output couplings, refute uniqueness under those premises. If a unique attractor appears, test its basin, stability, and scale dependence before attempting a theorem. Physical length, time, and energy calibration must precede claims about dimensionful G; mere unit conversion does not select a coupling.

## 8. Dark matter

**Mechanism.** Some stable network excitations might carry an independently defined gravitational source while remaining weakly visible to electromagnetic probes. Another branch might alter large-scale response rather than introduce an additional matter population. These explanations must be kept distinct.

**Current support.** Neither branch follows from a retained-record count. The [dark-sector laboratory](DARK_SECTOR_CONSTRUCTION_LAB_V001.md) shows why densities and ratios remain adjustable without native production, energy, stress, and exchange laws. Dust-like dilution is a useful property, not a complete dark-matter identification.

**Test.** Define a candidate population and derive its stored energy and transport before calling it dark. Establish an interaction contrast with electromagnetic probes and an independently defined source/response contrast; absence from one readout alone proves only invisibility to that readout. Vary initial populations to test whether abundance is selected or freely normalized. A modified-response branch must instead predict a source-dependent departure with the added-population alternative held fixed, in the stated effective domain.

## 9. Dark energy and the cosmological constant

**Mechanism.** Collective network stress, a state-dependent energy functional, or an alternative large-scale response could potentially account for accelerated expansion. Maintenance is a rate of activity or power expenditure; it becomes such a mechanism only through a derived stress and energy budget.

**Current support.** The construction laboratory proves that constant density can describe either a separately conserved negative-pressure component or replenished dust with a donor. Its explicit dust-token/radiation maintenance candidate cannot accelerate the assumed Einstein-FRW branch. That rejects a concrete shortcut, not every record-based alternative. The current conditional endpoint does not select Lambda's value or establish its stability against changes in microscopic contributions.

**Test.** Derive pressure and all transfer channels from one candidate, then test reservoir exhaustion and sensitivity to allowed energy-reference/constitutive choices. Distinguish an unobservable finite-system energy-reference shift from a physical metric-dependent vacuum term; their identification needs an action/source map. A non-GR branch may evade the GR acceleration criterion, but must derive its replacement expansion/source equation and observable consequences rather than borrowing the conclusion.

## 10. Horizons, black-hole entropy, and information

**Mechanism.** Limited access across a network boundary could relate accessible information to boundary channels rather than bulk labels. Dynamical trapping could define an operational horizon. These ideas could connect record capacity, causal access, and entropy if one construction supports all three.

**Current support.** The adopted RGRL record explicitly withholds singularity resolution and a black-hole S-matrix. A finite state space bounds information capacity, but neither that fact nor homological record counting implies a black-hole entropy law, evaporation dynamics, or preservation/loss of information in a gravitational process.

**Test.** Freeze a boundary-access protocol and count distinguishable states as interior size and boundary channel capacity vary independently. Compare constrained states with unconstrained mixed-state controls; a boundary law inserted through the state ensemble is not a derived universal law. Then test whether the native dynamics actually traps the corresponding probes. Until a physical area, energy, temperature, and gravitational horizon are jointly earned, interpret the result as a finite accessibility law. Directly discrete horizon alternatives may be tested at that ceiling.

## 11. What unification would mean

The strongest network explanation would derive several independent observables from one unchanged parent, with the same parameters, conserved budget, and scale map. Separate graphs tuned to reproduce separate phenomena would constitute a collection of models, not yet a unified mechanism.

Three shared constructions have particular leverage:

| Shared construction | Questions it could connect | First decisive contrast |
|---|---|---|
| Native record energy, stress, and exchange | Inertia/gravity source, dark populations, maintenance pressure, abundance selection | A complete budget and stress prediction versus freely adjustable bookkeeping |
| Influence structure, clocks, and independent transport probes | Causality, locality, dimension, common metric, horizons | Agreement on one operational geometry versus probe/scheduler-dependent descriptions |
| Protected state structure plus dynamics | Stable records, matter-like excitations, charge, memory-dependent response | Persistence and transport under intervention versus static topology or an inserted constraint |

A practical route is to choose one small parent and require a prediction in a second observable family after fixing it using the first. Keep a comparison model with the proposed causal ingredient removed and ordinary alternatives matched. Success would strengthen the connection actually tested; failure would identify which proposed bridge to replace. This creates justified confidence before a complete proof without treating repeated demonstrations of the same identity as independent evidence.

The current finite record results and L4 operational response make the lineage branch the best-supported starting point inside UNT. The energy/stress and common-probe constructions connect the most outstanding questions. Their importance is that one answer can constrain several explanations—not that the network hypothesis already guarantees a favorable answer.
