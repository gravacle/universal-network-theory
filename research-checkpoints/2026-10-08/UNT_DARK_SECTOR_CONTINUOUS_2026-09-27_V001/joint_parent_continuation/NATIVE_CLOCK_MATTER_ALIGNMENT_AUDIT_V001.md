## Rotation and sign restrictions

A normalized-gradient clock is hypersurface orthogonal. A vortical rotating
matter flow cannot coincide with it everywhere on a domain where that
vorticity is nonzero. A realistic multistream/dispersion-supported disk
also needs its owned stress and current description. Exact alignment cannot
be imposed globally by choosing a large zeta or by silently removing the
disk's rotation. Partial response is possible but requires solving the
actual momentum and clock equations.

Near a cold aligned rest background, let w be the small clock velocity and
v_b the matter velocity. The new quadratic term is

    L_align,2=-(zeta rho/2)|v_b-w|^2.

The ordinary matter kinetic term is positive rho |v_b|^2/2. At fixed clock
the combined coefficient is therefore rho(1-zeta)/2, not rho(1+zeta)/2.
Zeta>1 fails this elementary matter kinetic test; zeta=1 is degenerate.
For T=t+pi, w=-grad pi, so the same expression also changes the clock
gradient and matter-clock mixing. A value 0<zeta<1 is NOT a full stability
certificate: the original constrained kinetic system still needs analysis.

The exact cold alignment contribution measured by the clock has energy
-zeta rho gamma^2(gamma^2-1)/2. Together with ordinary dust, that sector's
clock-frame energy is rho gamma^2[1-zeta(gamma^2-1)/2]. For any positive
zeta this becomes negative at sufficiently large relative gamma. Thus the
displayed action is not a globally positive-energy penalty; a restricted
effective domain or a separately specified healthy completion would be
additional content. This calculation alone is not a theorem about instability
of every weakly misaligned solution of the full gravitational system.

A baryon-specific coupling is also not automatically universal for photons
or other matter. Preferred-clock effects, equivalence-principle tests and
all applicable matter species must be assessed explicitly.
