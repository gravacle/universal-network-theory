## Exact pair, without removing the contact or energy

Replace the single hard atom of total weight beta and gap R by two identical
atoms of weight beta/2. This explicitly adopts a two-copy degeneracy and
adds a relative degree of freedom; it is NOT a canonical rewriting of the
old single atom. Its summed static spectral weight and potential parameters
are unchanged, while its state space and preparation capacity are enlarged.
The declared pair Lagrangian is

    L_H = (beta/2) sum_{s=+,-}
          [ |dot y_s|²/2 - R²|y_s|²/2 - |y_s|⁴/4 + R h·y_s ].

Define the collective coordinate p=(y_++y_-)/2 and the antisymmetric
coordinate z=(y_+-y_-)/2. This invertible change of coordinates gives exactly

    L_H = beta { (|dot p|²+|dot z|²)/2
          - R²(|p|²+|z|²)/2
          - (|p|²+|z|²)²/4 - (p·z)² + R h·p }.       (1)

Thus the signed source polarization is B_H=beta R p, not an orbit average
assigned by hand. The positive kinetic and potential energy of z remain.
For a prescribed homogeneous expanding background the exact equations are

    pddot+3H pdot+R²p+(|p|²+|z|²)p+2(p·z)z = R h,
    zddot+3H zdot+R²z+(|p|²+|z|²)z+2(p·z)p = 0.      (2)

In general these equations use the projected convective derivative and its
expansion, as in EXPLICIT_MODE_COSMOLOGY_PATHWAY.md. They are not free-field
equations in an arbitrarily prescribed galaxy metric.

At h=0, p=pdot=0 is an EXACT invariant subspace. It has B_H=dot B_H=0 while
z obeys the nonzero-energy even-potential oscillator. This removes the
specific coherent linear forcing of the old single-atom pulse at h=0.
It does not turn the full metric constraint into a unique or solved evolution.

At nonzero common h the pair does not remain strictly antipodal: p responds.
In the quadratic theory, however, the p and z equations separate exactly,
so a common field excites the collective polarization without directly
driving the antisymmetric stored-energy oscillator. In the finite quartic

## Controlled algebra of the hard-gap limit

At h=0 the antisymmetric mode's energy is

    E_H = beta[|dot z|²/2+R²|z|²/2+|z|⁴/4].           (3)

For a straight-line orbit its turning amplitude b satisfies

    b² = R²[sqrt(1+4E_H/(beta R⁴))-1]
       = 2E_H/(beta R²)+O(R^-6).                     (4)

This is the same cold-history oscillator energy law already derived; there
is no extra abundance. For fixed finite E_H and a bounded slowly varying h,
the leading stationary collective response is p=h/R. Substitution into the
quadratic collective action leaves +beta |h|²/2. Consequently the hard static
polarization/contact beta h SURVIVES R->infinity even though the free coherent
polarization of z cancels exactly. Dropping this term would alter both the
force calibration and the clock equations.

The quartic collective term scales as beta |h|⁴/R⁴ and the collective/stored
couplings as E_H |h|²/R⁴. The collective kinetic correction scales as
beta |dot h|²/R². These statements are fixed-amplitude, finite-time ordered
estimates, not a uniform early-universe theorem. Source frequencies, H and
metric rates must remain small compared with R in the same physical units.
Neither an arbitrarily rapid source nor all epochs satisfy this automatically.

For a straight-line stored oscillator, the leading averaged pressure is

    P_H/E_H = 3E_H/(8 beta R⁴)+O(R^-8),               (5)

with the appropriate finite-compression corrections from the exact action
law. At a fixed finite epoch the hard-gap limit has zero excitation pressure

Equation (1) works for spatial vectors. At finite R the term (p·z)² retains
