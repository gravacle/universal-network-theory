3. The source-weighted band has a two-dimensional linear-gap density of
   states, a specified ultraviolet cutoff, and the specified gap-independent
   dipole matrix element. An unweighted geometric DOS alone does not establish
   these source weights or the ground-energy law.
4. The isotropic linear source port is a four-state singlet--triplet block
   described below. A scalar two-state analogy is not its covariant derivation.
5. A single universal critical calibration is imposed. It is an effective
   calibration, not yet a native explanation of exact criticality.
6. Each band block occupies its passive ground state for the static response
   calculation. Preparation, coldness, finite-time following and absolute
   reference energy require a formation and energy budget.
7. The dipole is encoded as fixed physical mass times material displacement,
   so its matrix element scales as length. This is load-bearing and differs
   from an energy-scaled native dipole.
8. The actual covariant source coupling, full material and clock kinetics,
   anisotropic band stress, ordinary clock/ruler map and gravitational action

## Source block and cold static response

    sum_modes lambda_Delta^2/I_Delta < h,  c=1.

Equivalently, with clamped static susceptibility weights and bare poles,

    sum_modes chi_Delta Omega_0,Delta^2 < 4 pi G h.

This condition uses the bare, clamped poles. Freely coupled dressed poles
can become fast near a kinetic degeneracy and are not substitutes for this

For the bright singlet--triplet mode near its ground state, eliminating the
conjugate quantum amplitude gives I_Delta=hbar^2/Delta,

    Lambda^2 < 3 pi G h hbar^2,  c=1.

If h is expressed as physical energy density while accelerations and
velocities retain SI units, the corresponding bound is
Lambda^2 < 3 pi G h hbar^2/c^2. An apparently fast cold band can conflict
with cosmological material inertia in this particular realization.
