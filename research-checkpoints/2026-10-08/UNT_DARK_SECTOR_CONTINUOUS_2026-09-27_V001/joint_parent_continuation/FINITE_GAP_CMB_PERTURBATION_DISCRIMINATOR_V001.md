## 2. A direct CMB distinction survives background degeneracies

Primary analyses establish that fluid radiation and free-streaming radiation
with the same homogeneous density have different perturbation signatures.
The former has no anisotropic stress; the latter requires a multipole
hierarchy. Their acoustic phase/amplitude responses differ. See the explicit
forward-model comparison in [Brinckmann et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2212.13264v2),
sections III–IV. This is guidance on which equations to solve, not a posterior
bound imported into the present model.

In an epoch after electron annihilation, before the hard transition, with
massless ordinary neutrinos and photons obeying their usual thermal relation,
our adopted background alone fixes the free-streaming radiation fraction:

    fnu=(7/8)(4/11)^(4/3),
    R_free = fnu N_eff,ordinary /
                     [1+fnu N_eff,ordinary+fnu Delta_H].

Here Delta_H is the same asymptotic energy coefficient used in the BBN proxy,
not the finite-epoch hard density divided by photon density after it becomes
matter-like. This expression does not apply across electron annihilation or
to massive-neutrino epochs without the corresponding thermal/kinetic history.
For fixed ordinary neutrinos, extra fluid radiation decreases this fraction;
adding free-streaming radiation instead would increase it. The asymptotic
radiation-era phase response therefore cannot be assigned the sign or
amplitude of an added-neutrino template merely by matching H.

The relation between free-streaming propagation and acoustic phase is derived
in [Bashinsky and Seljak](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0310198).
Actual observed TT/TE/EE extrema also depend on baryon loading, time-dependent
potentials, projection, the visibility function and primordial spectral shape;
[Pan et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.03091) explicitly evaluate these effects.
No asymptotic phase coefficient is substituted here for a measured peak shift.

For a finite gap, the new population transitions during its history. A mode
entering well before that transition samples fluid radiation; a mode entering
well afterwards samples nearly pressureless clustering; intermediate modes
have a scale-dependent transfer. It is not enough to apply a constant-fluid
radiation limit either. For example, [ACT's extended-model analysis](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.14454v2)
specifies a constant relativistic-fluid component and its associated parameter
assumptions. Its marginalized limit is not a likelihood for this changing
single-component history. The published observational spectra remain useful
data once this model's complete forward calculation is supplied.
