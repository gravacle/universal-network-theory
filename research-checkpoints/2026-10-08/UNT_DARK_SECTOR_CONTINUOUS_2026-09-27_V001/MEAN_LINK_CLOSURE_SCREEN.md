## Question and native scope

Can the same fixed coherent carrier Hamiltonian reproduce both the phase and
the transfer probability generated by a fluctuating link?

The readable one-link BS source has a hold Hamiltonian of the form

\[
H=H_B\otimes I-j\,n\otimes X+I\otimes D,
\qquad n^2=n,
\]

where B contains writer, record and link; D is diagonal in carrier left/right
basis. The actual source's single-carrier energy is a scalar special case.
Its current-squared term belongs in H_B since the carrier current obeys J²=I.
All record-link feedback, link flipping, degree penalties and storage energy
remain in H_B. We do not assume `[H_B,n]=0`.

Specify a **fresh independent probe**:
`rho(0)=rho_B tensor |L><L|`, `p=Tr(rho_B n)`. This is a new preparation premise,
not a statement about the correlated carrier left over from the old formation
run. Tracing out that old carrier and replacing it is only a mathematical
comparator here; an actual reset/injection would need preparation/work accounting.

## Prediction before any new evolution

Writing t for a dimensionless hold duration with hbar=1, direct expansion of
the unitary gives

\[
(\rho_C)_{RL}=+ijpt+O(t^2),\qquad
P_R(t)=j^2p\,t^2+O(t^3).
\]

The first formula follows by tracing `-i[H,rho(0)]`. For the second, because
the initial state has zero right support, the only order-t² population term
is `Tr(P_R H rho(0) H)`; only the hopping part changes carrier position, and
`n²=n` gives the coefficient j²p. H_B and the diagonal carrier terms cannot
contribute at that order.

Any deterministic time-independent Hermitian 2x2 carrier-only Hamiltonian
matching the first derivative must have its off-diagonal matrix element equal
to -jp. It consequently predicts

\[
P_R^{\rm coh}(t)=j^2p^2t^2+O(t^3).
\]

The leading gap is therefore

\[
P_R-P_R^{\rm coh}=j^2p(1-p)t^2+O(t^3).
\]

Equivalently the native reduced purity is

\[
\operatorname{Tr}\rho_C(t)^2
=1-2j^2p(1-p)t^2+O(t^3).
\]

Thus **for j nonzero and 0<p<1, no such coherent carrier-only Hamiltonian can
match both signatures**. Fitting hopping to j sqrt(p) fixes the probability
coefficient but misses the measured first derivative. Fitting it to jp fixes
that derivative but misses probability. Diagonal carrier energy cannot repair
this leading discrepancy. At p=0 or 1 the leading obstruction vanishes; this
does not guarantee coherent closure at higher orders when link dynamics act.

## Why this helps the gravity/dark-sector construction

The simplest mean-link substitution is not a generally valid operational
geometry for microscopic probes. This identifies what the effective description
must retain or justify neglecting: link fluctuations, conditional states and
possibly reduced nonunitary/memory terms.

It is **not** a no-go theorem for stochastic geometry, quantum geometry, a
larger retained-field description, or a controlled long-wavelength/long-time
metric limit. An ordinary environment coupled to a probe gives the same effect;
this does not uniquely establish UNT. A fluctuating-metric description may
also produce nonunitary reduced dynamics. No pressure, attraction, stress tensor,
abundance, or observational dark-sector result follows from this screen alone.

The next constructive question is whether the native parent supplies a band
where those fluctuation effects can be bounded relative to a shared metric
prediction—not whether one can fit a different geometry to each observable.
