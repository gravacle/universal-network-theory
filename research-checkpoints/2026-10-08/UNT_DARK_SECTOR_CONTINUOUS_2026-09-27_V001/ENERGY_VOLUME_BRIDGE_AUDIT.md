The native source offers a smaller genuine step toward the required common-probe interpretation. Let \(B\) contain writer, record, and link, and let \(C\) be the two-state carrier. \[

\qquad j=t_{\rm hop},
\]

where

\[

\[

\qquad C_0=2U_dd_*^2+\epsilon_\psi.
\]

Here \(C_0\) is only a finite-cell Hamiltonian constant, not the cosmological action coefficient \(C_0^{\rm eff}\) above. The source has no nontrivial independent carrier diagonal energy; adding such a diagonal term would not change the leading coefficients below. Record and writer storage terms remain in \(H_B\).

This reduction confirms that changing a named 'current-squared feedback' coefficient in this two-state cell is precisely a link-bias change. It also identifies the actual operator through which this cell can carry an independent probe, without introducing a volume map.

For a fresh product preparation \(\rho_B\otimes|L\rangle\langle L|\), put \(p=\operatorname{Tr}(n\rho_B)\). Direct expansion, without assuming \([H_B,n]=0\), gives

\[

\[

It therefore predicts \(P_R=j^2p^2t^2+O(t^3)\). Its missing transfer coefficient is \(j^2\operatorname{Var}(n)\), and unitary carrier-only evolution cannot reproduce the purity loss. For \(0<p<1\) and \(j\ne0\), the deterministic mean-link replacement already fails this short-time test. At \(p=0\) or \(1\), this leading test is inconclusive about later evolution; \(H_B\) can subsequently generate link fluctuations.
