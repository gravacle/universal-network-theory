## An interaction follows from the existing network dynamics

The native action has no direct record-pair term. Nevertheless, its conditional
graph ground energy depends non-additively on the record pattern. Its exact
occupation-energy Hessian is `-lambda_R^2 chi`, where chi is the graph's static
degree susceptibility. This is a derived effective interaction, not an added
microscopic pair force.

At finite positive link-flip amplitude, chi has rank 2N-1 on the complete
bipartite possible-link set. Its sole null direction increases every left
record-control field while decreasing every right field by the same amount.
That cancellation follows from equal layer degree totals, not a spacetime
gauge symmetry. These control fields are a differentiation device; actual
record occupations remain binary.

For one native link, the two-record insertion cross-cost is strictly negative.
In a stated illustrative admissible parameter set it is exactly `1-sqrt(2)`.
Both individual insertions still cost positive energy, with all storage and
graph offsets retained. This is an attractive *energy comparison*, not yet a
force or a record-clustering trajectory.

## The sign is not universal, and that is a useful scientific constraint

One fixed admissible 2+2-node native Hamiltonian gives both signs. In an
occupied opposite-layer background, two same-layer records compete for a
favorable link endpoint. Their binary cross-cost is positive. An opposite-layer
pair on the blank background has a negative cross-cost. The backgrounds differ
explicitly; this is not an assumed pairwise-additive vacuum potential.

The proof minimizes over all graph configurations, not over an imposed
matching subset. Restoring all four native flips at h=1/64 gives exact bounds

    same-layer cross-cost:      [ 1/4,  3/4],
    opposite-layer cross-cost: [-3/4, -1/4].

## Real-time incidence responds, but does not automatically equilibrate

An exact one-link comparison starts every record branch with the same graph
state. Its link expectation responds to changed record feedback by an explicitly
derived oscillation. It does not simply become the conditional ground value.
The graph susceptibility has a nonzero finite-frequency response, even though
the fixed-label record occupations themselves remain conserved during the hold.
The complete branch energy and its excitation above the conditional ground
are retained; there is no free write, dropped excitation or reset energy zero.

The same one-link native carrier Hamiltonian also has a clear limitation:
enhancing that single link gives equal inclusive carrier transfer probabilities
in both directions for the same initial graph state. Putting the record at
one endpoint does not by itself select motion toward that endpoint. Carrier
backreaction was retained in the symmetry argument.

This is why the static binding result cannot be promoted directly to gravity.
