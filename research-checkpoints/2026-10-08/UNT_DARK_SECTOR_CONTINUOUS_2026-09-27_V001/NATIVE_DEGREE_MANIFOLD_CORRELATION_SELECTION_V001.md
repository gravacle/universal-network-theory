## 1. Declared restriction and exact completion of the square

Fix one slab of N+N nodes; no carriers; all formation couplings off; uniform fixed record occupation q∈{0,1}; and no graph-dependent ports. Other retained factors contribute the same declared constant. Let U=U_d>0, h=Ω/N>0, a=Δ−2λ_Rq, and choose an integer target degree d0 with 0≤d0≤N. The graph Hamiltonian is

\[
 H=U\sum_v(d_v-d_*)^2+a\sum_e n_e-h\sum_eX_e+E_{\rm rest}.
\]

Declare the parameter relation

\[
 d_*=d_0+{a\over4U}.
\]

Since Σ_v d_v=2Σ_e n_e, this gives exactly

\[
 H=C+H_0+V,\qquad
 H_0=U\sum_v(d_v-d_0)^2,\quad V=-h\sum_eX_e,
\]
\[
 C=Na d_0+{Na^2\over8U}+E_{\rm rest}.                                  \tag{1}
\]

No energy reference is discarded or reset between graph states. The BS text treats its displayed microscopic coefficients as real and states no integer restriction on d_*; d0, not d_*, must be an allowed integer graph degree. This tuning is a newly declared subfamily, not a parameter relation derived from formation or active/maintenance accounting. It must be fixed once, not retuned across q histories. The other BS stability/completion conditions remain separate obligations; e.g. its sufficient Δ bound can be retained while choosing U and this real d_*.

Let P project onto all labeled d0-regular bipartite graphs and Q=I−P. These graphs have Nd0 edges. All have H_0=0, and every single link flip costs 2U. The full unperturbed gap above P is 2U: any nonzero degree deviation has squared sum at least 2 because the two layer degree totals agree. This finite-N graph restriction is not a large-N sparse-phase construction.

## 2. Effective operator through fourth order

Set R=−QH_0^{-1}Q. Since no one- or two-distinct-flip move connects different fixed-degree graphs,

\[
 PVP=0,\qquad
 H_{\rm eff}^{(2)}=PVRVP=-{N^2h^2\over2U}P.                            \tag{2}
\]

All regular configurations have the same edge-number parity, so odd-order terms vanish. In the Hermitian effective-Hamiltonian convention, the fourth-order term is

\[
 H_{\rm eff}^{(4)}
 =PVRVRVRVP-\frac12
 \{PVR^2VP,\ PVRVP\},\qquad
 PVR^2VP={N^2h^2\over4U^2}P.                                          \tag{3}
\]

The second term, often called the folded contribution, is therefore the positive scalar N⁴h⁴/(8U³)P. It supplies no off-diagonal move.

### Alternating-square switch

The only nontrivial four-flip fixed-degree move replaces the two occupied diagonals of an alternating 2×2 rectangle by its two empty diagonals. There are 24 orderings. Sixteen have successive intermediate gaps (2U,2U,2U); eight have (2U,4U,2U). Every intermediate state is outside P. Thus each switch has amplitude

\[
 -h^4\left[{16\over(2U)^3}
          +{8\over(2U)(4U)(2U)}\right]
 =-{5h^4\over2U^3}.                                                   \tag{4}
\]

This is generated by four applications of the original single-link flip, not by adding a microscopic loop interaction.

### Diagonal terms

A nonfolded diagonal length-four path uses two distinct edges twice each. Of their six words, four avoid returning to P after the second flip. For an unordered edge pair, the two-flip gap is 2U if incident edges have opposite occupations, 6U if incident edges have equal occupations, and 4U if they are disjoint. Their counts in any d0-regular graph are

\[
 A=2Nd_0(N-d_0),\qquad
 B=N^2(N-1)-A,\qquad
 D={N^2(N-1)^2\over2}.                                                \tag{5}
\]

These counts depend on N and d0, not graph topology. The nonfolded diagonal contribution is −(h⁴/U³)(A/2+B/6+D/4). Adding the folded term gives the constant

\[
 D_4={h^4\over U^3}
 \left[{N^2(2N+1)\over24}-{2N\over3}d_0(N-d_0)\right].                 \tag{6}
\]

If A_sw is the unweighted adjacency matrix of allowed alternating-square switches between regular labeled graphs, the complete result is

\[
 H_{\rm eff}
 =\left(C-{N^2h^2\over2U}+D_4\right)P
 -{5h^4\over2U^3}A_{\rm sw}
 +O_N(h^6/U^5).                                                       \tag{7}
\]

The scalar fourth-order term is not proportional to each graph's number of allowed switches. Therefore (7) is not a classical Markov generator with a compensating switch-degree diagonal. The correlation selection below is a quantum ground-state statement.

## 3. Finite-N validity and its limitations

The native perturbation has exact norm v=||V||=hN². A conservative sufficient isolation condition is

\[
 \eta={hN^2\over U}={\Omega N\over U}<1.                               \tag{8}
\]

The perturbed low band then remains separated from the rest by at least 2U−2v>0. A resolvent contour of radius U also gives finite-N analytic perturbation theory for ||V||<U. Taking η≪1 controls the expansion; (7)'s remainder notation has N-dependent constants and is not a uniform large-N bound.

For the normalized exact graph-only ground state ψ, its energy after subtracting C is ≤0 by a regular-graph trial state. Its excited-manifold component obeys the additional conservative bound

\[
 \|Q\psi\|\le {v\over2U-v}\|P\psi\|
 ={\eta\over2-\eta}\|P\psi\|.                                         \tag{9}
\]

This controls degree-violating admixture, not ground-state preparation. P is an analytical low-energy projector here, not a claimed physical postselection protocol. No cooling, equilibration, or suitable timescale is inferred from a closed source-off hold.

## 4. Exact N=4, d0=2 orbit reduction

There are only two unlabeled regular types: C8 and two disjoint C4s. The labeled counts are

\[
 n_C={(4!)^2\over8}=72,\qquad
 n_S=3\cdot3\cdot2=18.
\]

Each C8 graph has 12 allowed switches: 8 stay in the C8 class and 4 enter the two-square class. Each two-square graph has 16 switches, all entering the C8 class. Edge balance is 72·4=18·16. With normalized uniform orbit vectors |C⟩ and |S⟩, the switch quotient is

\[
 A_{\rm sw}\big|_{\rm invariant}
 =\begin{pmatrix}8&8\\8&0\end{pmatrix}.                               \tag{10}
\]

For this case (5) gives A=32, B=16, D=72. The nonfolded diagonal is −(110/3)h⁴/U³ and the folded term is +32h⁴/U³, yielding D_4=−(14/3)h⁴/U³.

The largest quotient eigenvalue is 4+4√5. Its positive eigenvector has orthonormal-orbit amplitude ratio

\[
 {b_{\rm orbit}\over a_{\rm orbit}}={\sqrt5-1\over2}.
\]

The per-labeled-graph coefficient ratio differs:

\[
 {b_{\rm state}\over a_{\rm state}}=\sqrt5-1,
\]

because √(72/18)=2. Thus the weak-flip ground-selection limit is

\[
 \lim_{h/U\to0^+}\Pr(S)={1-1/\sqrt5\over2},\qquad
 \lim_{h/U\to0^+}\langle\widehat C_4\rangle=1-{1\over\sqrt5},             \tag{11}
\]

where the native square count is

\[
 \widehat C_4=\sum_{i<k,\ j<l}n_{ij}n_{il}n_{kj}n_{kl}.
\]

The probability in (11) is valid both for the exact state in the limit and for its normalized P projection in that limit. At fixed N, the isolated quartic eigenbranch in the permutation-invariant sector gives corrections O_N((h/U)²), from higher effective terms and state dressing; no numerical error coefficient is certified here.

The orbit-constant eigenvector is strictly positive on all 90 regular graphs, so its eigenvalue is the full switch adjacency's spectral radius even without invoking a separate connectivity theorem for that switch graph. More importantly, the full finite graph-only Hamiltonian with h>0 has negative flip matrix elements on the connected bit hypercube. Its ground state is unique and strictly positive by the finite Perron–Frobenius argument, and is invariant under the exact within-layer permutations. Hence the two-orbit reduction captures its weak-flip selection. No analogous claim is made for the full coupled BS parent.

## 5. Exact homogeneous means coexist with selected correlations

At d0=N/2=2, global link complementation F=∏_eX_e sends d_v to N−d_v and preserves both H_0 and V. Uniqueness makes the graph-only ground state invariant under F. Therefore

\[
 \langle n_e\rangle=\frac12\qquad
 \text{for every edge and every }h>0,                                 \tag{12}
\]

not merely to fourth order. BS16's mean-link graph is consequently the same weighted K_(4,4) for all h in this tuned subfamily, while (11) displays a nontrivial weak-flip selection of graph correlations. Relative to the uniform mixture over the 90 regular labeled configurations, whose two-square weight is 1/5, the limiting ground state weights that disconnected class more heavily. This finite comparison is neither a large-N geometric phase nor a universal preference for fragmentation.

The constructive result is that native degree penalties plus native link flips can select nontrivial graph correlations without an added microscopic loop term. It does not show that the actual retained-record history reaches the ground state, that the selected correlations give physical locality, or that they explain dark-sector effects. For example, the maximally mixed regular-manifold state remains maximally mixed under the leading effective switch Hamiltonian; unitary evolution alone does not turn it into (11). A future test must keep this subfamily and its preparation/control assumptions fixed and check the correlation prediction, rather than fitting mean links or declaring ground preparation after the fact.
